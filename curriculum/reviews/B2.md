## Findings and fixes

- b2-20: *Está de camarero* uses the Spain word for "waiter" → changed to *mesero* (Latin American).
- b2-20: the ser/estar table included *no está muy católico* and *está negro* ("furious"), which are mainly colloquial Spain usage → removed; replaced with the widely used pairs *listo* (clever / ready) and *aburrido* (boring / bored).
- b2-16 and b2-20: *guapa* ("pretty") is more typical of Spain → examples now use *linda*, the everyday Latin American word, and the b2-20 answer key still accepts *guapa/bonita*.
- b2-16: *Ya verás lo bien que lo pasamos* is Spain-style → *Ya verás lo bien que la vamos a pasar* (*pasarla bien*, Latin American, and future meaning made explicit).
- b2-05: two-speaker exchanges (*¿Por qué no ha venido? Se habrá dormido.*) had no dialogue dashes → added dashes in the Spanish and the English.
- b2-10: *He querido siempre que seas feliz* is unnatural word order → *Siempre he querido que seas feliz.*
- b2-13: *No dudo que ___ verdad* accepted only *es*; the subjunctive *sea* is also correct → both accepted, and the explanation now notes that *no dudar* usually takes the indicative.
- b2-19: the *con* row listed *soñar con* twice → duplicate removed.
- b2-19: the note on dropping *de* before *que* mislabelled the error → now names it *queísmo* and contrasts it correctly with *dequeísmo*.
- b2-29: the multiple-choice item on *él/el* printed "(él)" in the prompt, giving the answer away → prompt rewritten without the hint.
- b2-29: the four spelling items shared one prompt, and labelling them with the target word would reveal the answer → each now asks for the word by its English meaning.
- b2-16, b2-21, b2-32: English labels in fill-in prompts ("a woman", "diminutive", "conditional") → removed from prompts; the English goes in the hint.

## Checked and found correct (no change)

- The indicative/subjunctive "assertion" principle and every paired example in b2-01; *aunque / a pesar de que / por mucho que* with both moods; time clauses with *en cuanto / apenas / hasta que / siempre que*, including the past-future imperfect subjunctive.
- Pluperfect subjunctive and conditional perfect in *si* clauses (never conditional after *si*; *hubiera* accepted in the result clause), mixed conditionals, future perfect and conditional perfect of probability.
- Conditions without *si* (*con tal de que, a menos que, en caso de que, sin que*) always with the subjunctive; *de* + infinitive; *como si* / *ni que* only with imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive.
- Relatives (*el que, el cual, lo cual, quien, cuyo* agreeing with the thing possessed), prepositions before relatives, and sequence of tenses tables.
- Reporting verbs split by mood, passive forms (*ser / estar* + participle agreement, passive *se*), negated opinion verbs and *no es que*, cause and consequence connectors, verbal periphrases (*llevar* + participle agreement), nominalisation with *lo*.
- Formal-letter formulas (colon after the salutation, *A quien corresponda*, *reclamo* as the LatAm complaint word), economy and politics vocabulary, idioms (regional ones labelled with their country), accent rules and the plural accent shifts (*jóvenes, canciones*), quantifiers (*cualquier / cualquiera*, *mil millones* = US billion), voseo forms (*sos, tenés, querés, vení, decime*) and the regional vocabulary table.

## Limits

naturalness and regional-usage issues; the answer keys, however, were checked item by item,
and wherever a regional variant exists the lesson either labels it or accepts the
