## Findings and fixes

- c1-12: the multiple-choice item treated *Es Juan quien lo dijo* as wrong and the explanation said *ser* "agrees" with the main verb; in real usage the present is also heard → the explanation now says *ser* "usually matches", and the options were changed so that only *Fue* fits (distractors *Será / Fuera / Sea*).
- c1-29: a two-speaker exchange (*¿Por qué no viniste? Porque estaba enfermo.*) had no dialogue dashes → dashes added in the example, the translation and the fill-in item.
- c1-08: the note said *imprimido / impreso* are both accepted "in LatAm"; the RAE accepts both everywhere → wording corrected.
- c1-01: a table row about *pensar que* + subjunctive was confusing and not really a meaning change → removed.
- c1-05: the note on *por si* + mood was muddled → rewritten: indicative (present, or imperfect in the past), with *por si* + imperfect subjunctive for a less likely case.
- c1-15: the slang lesson included a vulgar-leaning Mexican expression and a word that is offensive in some countries, neither of which a learner needs → removed; the lesson still warns that words like *coger* change meaning across borders.
- c1-28: one "joke" (*terremoto*) wasn't actually a working pun → removed.
- c1-25: the *víctima* item used English inside Spanish options → options are now Spanish-only, with the rule in the explanation.
- c1-01, c1-21, c1-27, c1-30: English labels inside fill-in prompts ("whatever", "literary", "very polite") → moved to the hint.
- c1-11, c1-13, c1-14: duplicate prompts and duplicate accepted answers → made distinct or de-duplicated.

## Checked and found correct (no change)

- Mood-driven meaning changes (*decir, sentir, comprender, temer, suponer, insistir en*) and every paired example; advanced concessives (*por + adj + que*, *aun + gerund*, *y eso que*, *así* + subjunctive, reduplicated subjunctive *digas lo que digas*); *aun / aún*.
- Conditions: *como* + subjunctive (threat) vs. *como* + indicative (cause), *de* + simple/compound infinitive, mixed conditionals, *yo que tú*.
- *cuyo* agreement including after prepositions, *el cual* after long prepositions, *lo que* vs. *lo cual* in free relatives, *-quiera* forms with the subjunctive.
- *de ahí que* + subjunctive, *por si* + indicative, *no sea que* + subjunctive, *a medida que / conforme / según* with future subjunctive, *tras*, *al* + infinitive, absolute participles with agreement, the formal-style rule against the "gerund of later event".
- Stylistic tenses (historical present, imperfect of politeness, conditional of rumour, future of concession), clitic doubling rules and leísmo/laísmo/loísmo labels (with the Latin American standard recommended), cleft sentences, advanced discourse markers, academic hedging, collocations, near-synonyms and false friends, prepositional verbs, mood after superlatives and *el único que* (both moods accepted in the answer keys), future subjunctive only for recognition, RAE 2010 spelling (*solo* without accent, *o* between numbers, *exnovio*), polite and condolence formulas.
- Regional labels on every slang item in c1-15 and on regional forms elsewhere (*¿me regala…?* Colombia, *albures* Mexico, *guagua* Cuba vs. Chile).

## Limits

Written and reviewed by the same system. C1 is where nuance and register matter most, so
this is the level where a native-speaker review would add the most; wherever usage is
genuinely divided, the lessons say so and the answer keys accept each correct variant.
