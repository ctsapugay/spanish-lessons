# French A1 — accuracy review

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-03, after all 30 A1 lessons were written. Lessons reviewed: a1-01,
a1-02, a1-03, a1-04, a1-05, a1-06, a1-07, a1-08, a1-09, a1-10, a1-11, a1-12, a1-13, a1-14,
a1-15, a1-16, a1-17, a1-18, a1-19, a1-20, a1-21, a1-22, a1-23, a1-24, a1-25, a1-26, a1-27,
a1-28, a1-29, a1-30.

## What was checked

1. **Spelling, gender and pronunciation, word by word** — `checks/french-accuracy.sh` run on
   every lesson: every French word (vocabulary, examples, italic French in explanations and
   tables, prompts, answers, French multiple-choice options) looked up in Lexique 3.83, every
   vocabulary noun's gender and every IPA guide compared with it. Final run: "30 lessons,
   10839 words spelled as in the lexicon, 498 vocabulary genders and pronunciation guides
   match". Words Lexique lacks are justified below under "Lexicon notes".
2. **Grammar and naturalness** — each lesson re-read in full: explanations (are the rules
   stated correctly and completely for A1?), examples (natural, current metropolitan French?),
   answer keys (does every typed exercise accept the usual correct variants — *tu/vous* where
   English "you" is ambiguous, *on/nous*, inversion / *est-ce que* / intonation questions?).
3. **Facts about France** — usage notes (tu/vous, *bonjour* in shops, café and restaurant
   customs, SNCF tickets, the Paris metro) checked against current practice.
4. **Teaching order** — `checks/french-content.sh`: nothing tested before it is taught.

## Findings and fixes

- a1-13: the example *Mon père est un bel homme* used *bel*, which the lesson never taught → added *beau → bel*, *vieux → vieil* before a vowel to the adjectives section.
- a1-17: the example *On déjeune à midi et demi* (correct: no *-e* after masculine *midi*) contradicted the lesson's *et demie* → added the rule *midi et demi*, *minuit et demi*.
- a1-27: the metro example gave *direction Mairie de Montrouge*, line 4's terminus only until 2022 (the line now runs to Bagneux) → changed to *direction Porte de Clignancourt*, the northern terminus.
- a1-20: the fill-in "Continuez ___'au feu" made learners type *jusqu* without its apostrophe → the blank is now the whole *jusqu'au*.
- a1-04, a1-06, a1-08, a1-13, a1-14, a1-15, a1-16, a1-20, a1-25, a1-30: several multiple-choice questions offered invented misspellings as wrong options (*canadiene*, *vendrice*, *mangons*, *gentile*, *blance*, *bonnée*, *faisez*, *étez*, *pouvent*, *fairé*…) → replaced by real forms (other persons, genders or numbers) or turned into typed exercises, so learners never see a misspelled word.
- a1-03, a1-07: *l'étudiant / l'étudiante* and *l'ami / l'amie* were given a single gender for both forms → written *un étudiant / une étudiante*, *un ami / une amie*, so each form shows its own gender.
- a1-07, a1-08: word endings (*-tion*, *-er*, *-ent*) were set in italics, which marks them as French words to speak → set in bold.
- a1-07: the guide for *il y a* /il.j‿a/ didn't match the dictionary transcription → /i.li.a/.
- a1-08: the exercises used forms (*habitez*, *écoutes*, *aime*, *étudie*, *regardent*) the lesson didn't show → added a line of example forms; a1-17 likewise for *se couche*; a1-28 for *les pieds*; a1-07 for *photos*.
- a1-09: a stray example (*il lut*, a literary past form) was confusing → removed. a1-20: a self-correcting sentence about *gauche* in the [ʒ] explanation → rewritten cleanly.
- a1-24: a multiple-choice option mixed English into French (*le pantalon (singular)*) → replaced by a question about *Mon pantalon est noir*.
- a1-04: *États-Unis* — Lexique transcribes it without the compulsory internal liaison → the guide keeps the standard /e.ta.zy.ni/, recorded as a lexicon note.

## Residual limits

The A1 French was written and reviewed by an AI, not a native speaker. Usage notes describe
standard metropolitan French; regional notes (Belgium, Switzerland, Québec) are short and
only where useful at A1.

## Lexicon notes

Words the course uses that Lexique 3.83 does not list (or lists without the needed
information), each with the reason it is correct. Checked by `checks/french-accuracy.sh`.

- `Camille` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Hugo` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Thomas` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Sophie` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Léa` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Martin` — the most common French surname (proper noun).
- `Paris` — the capital of France (proper noun).
- `cédille` /se.dij/ (f) — the cedilla, *la cédille*; missing from Lexique. Spelling, gender and pronunciation as in Le Robert and the Larousse dictionary.
- `h` /aʃ/ (m) — the name of the letter h (*le h*); Lexique lists only some letter names. Letter names are masculine.
- `Lyon` — French city (proper noun).
- `Ottawa` — the capital of Canada (proper noun).
- `Montréal` — Canadian city; the French spelling has the accent (proper noun).
- `Londres` — London, as it is called in French (proper noun).
- `Madrid` — the capital of Spain (proper noun).
- `France` /fʁɑ̃s/ (f) — country name, *la France*; Lexique has no proper nouns of countries.
- `Espagne` /ɛs.paɲ/ (f) — Spain, *l'Espagne*; feminine, so *en Espagne*.
- `Italie` /i.ta.li/ (f) — Italy, *l'Italie*; feminine, so *en Italie*.
- `Allemagne` /al.maɲ/ (f) — Germany, *l'Allemagne*; feminine, so *en Allemagne*.
- `Chine` /ʃin/ (f) — China, *la Chine*; feminine, so *en Chine*.
- `Canada` /ka.na.da/ (m) — the country, *le Canada*, *au Canada*. Lexique's lowercase *canada* (f) is a variety of apple, a different word.
- `Japon` /ʒa.pɔ̃/ (m) — Japan, *le Japon*, *au Japon*.
- `Portugal` /pɔʁ.ty.ɡal/ (m) — *le Portugal*, *au Portugal*.
- `Brésil` /bʁe.zil/ (m) — Brazil, *le Brésil*, *au Brésil*.
- `Mexique` /mɛk.sik/ (m) — Mexico, *le Mexique*, *au Mexique* (masculine despite the final -e).
- `États-Unis` /e.ta.zy.ni/ (m) — the United States, *les États-Unis*, *aux États-Unis*. Lexique writes /etayni/ without the compulsory liaison inside the compound; dictionaries (Le Robert, Larousse) give /etazyni/.
- `professeur` (m) — masculine noun; Lexique leaves its gender blank. The feminine is *professeure* (usual in France since the 2019 Académie française report on feminine job names).
- `médecin` (m/f) — *un médecin*; *une médecin* is also standard for a woman (Académie française, 2019).
- `journaliste` (m/f) — epicene noun: *un journaliste*, *une journaliste*.
- `livre` (m/f) — two nouns: *le livre* (the book) and *la livre* (the pound); Lexique leaves the gender blank because of this.
- `poste` (m/f) — two nouns: *la poste* (the post office, the post) and *le poste* (a job, a post; a set). Lexique leaves the gender blank because of this.
- `litre` (m) — *le litre*; Lexique leaves the gender blank. Masculine in all dictionaries.
- `huitante` /ɥi.tɑ̃t/ — 80 in parts of Switzerland (Vaud, Valais, Fribourg); mentioned only as a regional note. Missing from Lexique; listed in Le Robert and Larousse as a Swiss regionalism.
- `WC` /ve.se/ (m) — *les WC*, the toilet (from "water-closet"), always plural; an abbreviation, so not in Lexique.
- `Clignancourt` — *Porte de Clignancourt*, the northern terminus of Paris metro line 4 (proper noun).
- `ordonnance` (f) — *une ordonnance* (a prescription); Lexique leaves the gender blank. Feminine in all dictionaries.
- `Marseille` — French city (proper noun).
- `Nice` — French city (proper noun); Lexique's lowercase *nice* is an unrelated old adjective.
