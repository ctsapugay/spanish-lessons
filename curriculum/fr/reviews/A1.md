## Lexicon notes

Words the course uses that Lexique 3.83 does not list (or lists without the needed
information), each with the reason it is correct. Checked by `checks/french-accuracy.sh`.

- `Camille` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Hugo` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Thomas` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Sophie` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Léa` — a common French first name (proper noun).
- `Martin` — the most common French surname (proper noun).
- `Paris` — the capital of France (proper noun).
- `cédille` /se.dij/ (f) — the cedilla, *la cédille*; missing from Lexique. Spelling, gender and pronunciation as in Le Robert and the Larousse dictionary.
- `h` /aʃ/ (m) — the name of the letter h (*le h*); Lexique lists only some letter names. Letter names are masculine.
- `Lyon` — French city (proper noun).
- `Ottawa` — the capital of Canada (proper noun).
- `Montréal` — Canadian city; the French spelling has the accent (proper noun).
- `Londres` — London, as it is called in French (proper noun).
- `Madrid` — the capital of Spain (proper noun).
- `France` /fʁɑ̃s/ (f) — country name, *la France*; Lexique has no proper nouns of countries.
- `Espagne` /ɛs.paɲ/ (f) — Spain, *l'Espagne*; feminine, so *en Espagne*.
- `Italie` /i.ta.li/ (f) — Italy, *l'Italie*; feminine, so *en Italie*.
- `Allemagne` /al.maɲ/ (f) — Germany, *l'Allemagne*; feminine, so *en Allemagne*.
- `Chine` /ʃin/ (f) — China, *la Chine*; feminine, so *en Chine*.
- `Canada` /ka.na.da/ (m) — the country, *le Canada*, *au Canada*. Lexique's lowercase *canada* (f) is a variety of apple, a different word.
- `Japon` /ʒa.pɔ̃/ (m) — Japan, *le Japon*, *au Japon*.
- `Portugal` /pɔʁ.ty.ɡal/ (m) — *le Portugal*, *au Portugal*.
- `Brésil` /bʁe.zil/ (m) — Brazil, *le Brésil*, *au Brésil*.
- `Mexique` /mɛk.sik/ (m) — Mexico, *le Mexique*, *au Mexique* (masculine despite the final -e).
- `États-Unis` /e.ta.zy.ni/ (m) — the United States, *les États-Unis*, *aux États-Unis*. Lexique writes /etayni/ without the compulsory liaison inside the compound; dictionaries (Le Robert, Larousse) give /etazyni/.
- `professeur` (m) — masculine noun; Lexique leaves its gender blank. The feminine is *professeure* (usual in France since the 2019 Académie française report on feminine job names).
- `médecin` (m/f) — *un médecin*; *une médecin* is also standard for a woman (Académie française, 2019).
- `journaliste` (m/f) — epicene noun: *un journaliste*, *une journaliste*.
- `livre` (m/f) — two nouns: *le livre* (the book) and *la livre* (the pound); Lexique leaves the gender blank because of this.
- `poste` (m/f) — two nouns: *la poste* (the post office, the post) and *le poste* (a job, a post; a set). Lexique leaves the gender blank because of this.
- `litre` (m) — *le litre*; Lexique leaves the gender blank. Masculine in all dictionaries.
- `huitante` /ɥi.tɑ̃t/ — 80 in parts of Switzerland (Vaud, Valais, Fribourg); mentioned only as a regional note. Missing from Lexique; listed in Le Robert and Larousse as a Swiss regionalism.
