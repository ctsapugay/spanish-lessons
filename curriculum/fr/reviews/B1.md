## Findings and fixes

- b1-17: the passive table gave *La maison est construite en 2025* as "the house is being built", but *est construite* describes a finished state → replaced by *Le courrier est distribué le matin* (the post is delivered in the morning).
- all levels so far (a1-01, a2-05…a2-19, b1-02…b1-21; 112 places): bold inside italic French (*Je **les** ai achetées*) hid those words from the spelling check and from the record of what is taught → bold removed inside French; emphasis is given in the explanation instead. Once checked, *Donne-m'en* revealed that the spelling lookup didn't split hyphen + elision words → fixed in `scripts/frlex.py`.
- b1-08: *Je suis sûre qu'elle ___ raison* offered *aura eu* as a wrong option, but it can be grammatical → replaced by *aie*.
- b1-04: a wrong option was nonsense (*demain hier*) and another (*commande hier*) not a verb form → replaced with real wrong forms (*ira*, *commandera*).
- b1-03, b1-18: invented forms as options (*êtrais*, *fairant*, *avoirant*) → replaced by real forms.
- b1-23, b1-24: multiple-choice items on forming nouns and adverbs offered invented forms (*augmentement*, *lentment*, *prudentement*…) → turned into typed exercises, so learners only ever see correct forms.
- b1-11, b1-16: blanks that made learners type *qu* without its apostrophe → the blank is *qu'*.
- b1-25, b1-26: fill-in prompts contained English ("(satisfait, a woman)") → moved to the hint.
- b1-10, b1-15: capitalised emphasis inside French (*GÉ-nial*, *IN-croyable*) → the French is written normally and the emphasis described beside it.
- b1-28: liaisons shown with underscores inside spoken French (*Nous_avons*) → French written normally, liaisons shown beside it (nous‿avons).
- b1-11: an exercise used *demandent*, not yet shown → example added.
- b1-03, b1-12, b1-16, b1-21, b1-29: two vocabulary entries or prompts shared the same English (duplicate drills) → meanings distinguished.

## Lexicon notes

- `tour` (m/f) — two nouns: *le tour* (a turn, a trip round: *le tour du monde*, used here) and *la tour* (a tower); Lexique leaves the gender blank because of this.
- `USB` /y.ɛs.be/ (f) — *une clé USB* (a USB stick); an abbreviation said letter by letter, not in Lexique.
- `Grèce` /ɡʁɛs/ (f) — Greece, *la Grèce*, *en Grèce* (proper noun).
- `politique` (m/f) — two nouns: *la politique* (politics, policy — used here) and *le politique* (a politician, the political sphere); Lexique leaves the gender blank.
- `y` /j/ — before a vowel, the pronoun *y* is said as the glide [j]: *ça y est* [sa.jɛ], *il y a* [il.ja]; Lexique gives only the isolated form /i/.
- `gens` (m) — *les gens* (people), plural masculine (*des gens heureux*); Lexique leaves the gender blank (an adjective placed before it can take feminine forms in a few set phrases, *les vieilles gens*).
- `Monet` — Claude Monet, the painter (proper noun).
- `autrice` /o.tʁis/ (f) — feminine of *auteur*, recommended by the Académie française's 2019 report on feminine names of professions and now common; newer than Lexique.
- `gérondif` /ʒe.ʁɔ̃.dif/ (m) — the grammatical term (*le gérondif*: *en* + present participle); missing from Lexique, in all dictionaries.
- `Lille` — French city (proper noun).
- `nominalisation` /nɔ.mi.na.li.za.sjɔ̃/ (f) — the grammatical term, turning a verb into a noun; missing from Lexique, in Le Robert and Larousse.
- `mode` (m/f) — two nouns: *la mode* (fashion — used here) and *le mode* (a way, a mode; *le mode d'emploi*); Lexique leaves the gender blank.
- `soldes` (m) — *les soldes* (the sales), masculine plural (*des soldes intéressants*); Lexique leaves the gender blank.
- `recruteuse` /ʁə.kʁy.tøz/ (f) — feminine of *recruteur* (-eur → -euse); missing from Lexique, in Le Robert.
- `salariales` /sa.la.ʁjal/ — feminine plural of *salarial* (relating to pay): *prétentions salariales*, the fixed phrase for salary expectations; Lexique lacks this form.
- `Copenhague` /kɔ.pə.naɡ/ — Copenhagen, as it is called in French (proper noun).
