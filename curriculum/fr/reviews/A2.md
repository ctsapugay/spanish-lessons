# French A2 — accuracy review

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-03, after all 30 A2 lessons were written. Lessons reviewed: a2-01, a2-02, a2-03, a2-04, a2-05, a2-06, a2-07, a2-08, a2-09, a2-10, a2-11, a2-12, a2-13, a2-14, a2-15, a2-16, a2-17, a2-18, a2-19, a2-20, a2-21, a2-22, a2-23, a2-24, a2-25, a2-26, a2-27, a2-28, a2-29, a2-30.

## What was checked

1. **Spelling, gender and pronunciation** — `checks/french-accuracy.sh` on every lesson. Final
   run: "30 lessons, 12770 words spelled as in the lexicon, 440 vocabulary genders and
   pronunciation guides match". Words Lexique lacks or leaves without a gender are justified
   under "Lexicon notes".
2. **Grammar and naturalness** — every lesson re-read: rules stated correctly for A2 (passé
   composé agreement, imparfait formation and use, object pronouns and their positions, y / en,
   relatives, comparison, futur simple, negatives, imperative with pronouns, verb + à / de,
   possessive pronouns), examples natural in current metropolitan French, answer keys
   accepting the usual variants (*on / nous*, masculine / feminine speaker, inversion /
   *est-ce que*).
3. **Unambiguous questions** — every multiple-choice and fill-in item checked for a second
   correct answer a learner could reasonably give (especially passé composé vs imparfait).
4. **Facts about France** — school system, emergency numbers, contracts (CDI / CDD), housing
   ads, the festival calendar.
5. **Teaching order** — `checks/french-content.sh`.

## Findings and fixes

- a2-08: the liaison section listed *Quand | est-ce que* as a forbidden liaison; in fact *quand est-ce que* is linked /kɑ̃.t‿ɛs.kə/ → the forbidden case is now interrogative *quand* before an inverted verb (*Quand | êtes-vous arrivé ?*), with *quand est-ce que* given as the exception.
- a2-02, a2-03: two multiple-choice items ("Il ___ beau") accepted only the imparfait although *a fait* is also correct without context → each now has context that makes only the imparfait right (*pendant nos vacances… tous les jours*; *quand nous sommes arrivés*).
- a2-05: a fill-in hinted "I don't know her" but also accepted *le* → the key is now only *la*.
- a2-05: the example *Je les ai mises* shows participle agreement taught only at B1 → a short note now explains it and points ahead.
- a2-01, a2-10, a2-12: invented forms were offered as wrong options (*ouvri*, *disé*, *généreuxe*, *rouse*, *faira*, *allerai*, *avrai*, *venirai*…) → replaced by real forms of the same words.
- a2-07, a2-16: two vocabulary entries shared an English meaning (*allons-y* / *on y va*; *parfois* / *quelquefois*), which produced duplicate drills → meanings distinguished or entries merged.
- a2-08: a fill-in made learners type *qu* without its apostrophe → the blank is now *qu'*.
- a2-01: fill-in prompts contained English ("(sortir, a woman)") → the speaker's gender moved to the hint.
- a2-15: options mixing English "(nothing)" with French were marked as French → unmarked.
- a2-24: the explanation said *ma chef* while teaching *cheffe* → *mon chef / ma cheffe*.
- a2-02: *tous les jours* — Lexique gives *tous* only as /tus/ (the pronoun) → the determiner's /tu/ added as a lexicon note.
- a2-11: *devenu* and *se sont rencontrés* were tested but not shown → added to the explanation.

## Residual limits

The A2 French was written and reviewed by an AI, not a native speaker; colloquial forms
(*C'était pas mal*, *À plus*) are labelled as casual where they appear.

## Lexicon notes

- `tous` /tu/ — as a determiner (*tous les jours*) *tous* is pronounced /tu/; only the pronoun (*ils sont tous là*) is /tus/. Lexique lists only /tus/.
- `collègue` (m/f) — epicene noun: *un collègue*, *une collègue*; Lexique leaves the gender blank.
- `barbe` (f) — *la barbe* (the beard); Lexique leaves the gender blank. Feminine in all dictionaries.
- `pilote` (m/f) — epicene noun: *un pilote*, *une pilote*; Lexique leaves the gender blank.
- `Bernard` — a common French surname (proper noun).
- `Durand` — a common French surname, used for a company name (proper noun).
- `aide` (m/f) — two nouns: *l'aide* (f, help) and *un aide* (m, an assistant); Lexique leaves the gender blank. The lesson uses *l'aide*, feminine.
- `chef` (m) — *le chef*; Lexique leaves the gender blank.
- `cheffe` /ʃɛf/ (f) — the feminine of *chef*, recommended by the Académie française's 2019 report on feminine job names and now usual; newer than Lexique.
- `CDI` /se.de.i/ (m) — *contrat à durée indéterminée*, a permanent job contract; an abbreviation, said letter by letter.
- `CDD` /se.de.de/ (m) — *contrat à durée déterminée*, a fixed-term contract; an abbreviation.
- `CV` /se.ve/ (m) — *curriculum vitae*; an abbreviation.
- `Renault` — French car maker (proper noun).
- `Excel` — software name (proper noun).
- `élève` (m/f) — epicene noun: *un élève*, *une élève*; Lexique leaves the gender blank.
- `EPS` /ø.pe.ɛs/ (f) — *éducation physique et sportive*, PE at school; an abbreviation.
- `Bretagne` /bʁə.taɲ/ (f) — Brittany, *la Bretagne*, *en Bretagne* (proper noun).
- `Victor-Hugo` — a street name, after the writer Victor Hugo (proper noun).
- `propriétaire` (m/f) — epicene noun: *le / la propriétaire*; Lexique leaves the gender blank.
- `locataire` (m/f) — epicene noun: *le / la locataire*; Lexique leaves the gender blank.
- `poêle` (m/f) — two nouns: *la poêle* (the frying pan, used here) and *le poêle* (a stove); Lexique leaves the gender blank because of this.
